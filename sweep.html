What the spike is made of

The sweep is not miscellaneous. It is two literatures, pulled back to back.

At 21:31 the depth papers start — virtual logic depth, looped mixture-of-experts, gated recurrence, inference-time recirculation. At 21:33 the topic switches cleanly to zeroth-order and memory-constrained training. At 21:48 it returns to depth and picks up three algorithmic-limits papers on the way.

An hour and forty minutes before any of it, at 19:51, came Highly Opinionated Advice on How to Write ML Papers. Read in that order, the evening has an obvious shape: a contribution was decided on, and then the related work was assembled.

The gap between the two piles

Depth-recurrent models are parameter-light and compute-heavy. That is exactly the regime the memory-constrained training literature targets — and exactly the regime it never evaluates on.

Both literatures were saved ninety seconds apart. Neither cites the other. Two openings sit in the space between them:

  1. Recirculation and FoRo are the same move on different resources. Both freeze the pretrained weights and add capability — one adds serial computation, the other forward-only adaptation. Nobody has stacked them. A gated recurrent core's update gate is precisely the small, cheap-to-tune module that zeroth-order methods handle well and that backpropagation through R unrolled iterations handles badly.
  2. Looping changes the memory profile of backpropagation non-linearly. Backprop through R iterations of a shared block costs R× activation memory unless you recompute. MeSP's argument — recompute the cheap low-rank intermediate rather than store it — generalises directly to shared blocks, and neither paper mentions the other's setting. SMELT supplies the compute-matched protocol to evaluate it fairly.

The first is the more novel framing. The second is the more defensible empirical contribution.

Six clusters

Counts and date ranges are the collection's own; nothing here is a category invented to fill a row.

Depth as a resource separate from parameters

9 items · 2026-06-17 → 2026-09-07

The dominant thread, and near-complete for its scope. These are not nine papers on a topic — they are nine positions on one question: how much serial computation does a forward pass get, and can you buy more without buying more weights?

2506.18233
Beyond Parameters: Virtual Logic Depth. The thesis stated plainly — parameter reuse holds knowledge capacity near-constant while materially improving reasoning. Parameters ↔ knowledge; depth ↔ reasoning.
2609.01343
SMELT. The rigorous version. Loops the middle half of an MoE while matching per-token FLOPs, non-embedding parameters and KV cache; fits a separate Chinchilla law per architecture; 6.8–18.0% FLOP savings on the compute-optimal frontier. Fixes the standard cheat of comparing at fixed model size. This is the methodology to match.
2608.17981
Recirculation (Mozer et al., DeepMind). The strongest item here. Inference-time recurrence on frozen off-the-shelf models, framed as letting the network act as a dynamical system tracking belief states — explicitly distinct from both chain-of-thought and looping. Near-zero added generation latency; serial cost confined to prefill.
2608.15062
Gated Recurrent Transformers. Fixed prelude and coda blocks bracketing one shared core iterated R times, with a GRU-style update gate so the shared block can specialise per iteration. Directly addresses why naive depth-sharing collapses representational diversity.
2608.09888
BDH-CQ. Recurrent latent reasoning without verbalising: 150M parameters, 29.5% pass@2 on ARC-AGI-1 at $0.0007 per task. The existence proof that latent serial compute beats token-serial compute on cost.
2512.24880
mHC (DeepSeek). The plumbing. Hyper-Connections widened the residual stream and broke the identity-mapping property; mHC projects the connection space onto a manifold to restore it. What makes deep and heavily-recirculated stacks trainable at scale.
2606.19348
DeepSeek-V4. mHC in production, beside hybrid compressed-sparse attention and Muon. Evidence the residual-stream work is not academic.
2603.15339
The Neuroscience of Transformers — and the cortical-columns chapter beside it. A cortical column is a repeated shared block applied recurrently. Motivation paragraph, not evidence. Treat accordingly.

Gradient-free and memory-constrained training

Saved minutes after the depth papers. Also near-complete, and it has a clear internal argument: exact-but-heavy versus cheap-but-noisy, and the attempts to split the difference.

2006.06224

2402.15751

2404.08080
Variance-reduced Zeroth-Order Methods. The main lever on ZO's central weakness — estimate variance.
2602.13069
MeSP. The counter-position: don't go gradient-free, go structured. Hand-derived backward passes exploiting LoRA's low rank, recomputing h = xA rather than storing it. 49% memory cut with mathematically identical gradients — 361 MB → 136 MB on Qwen2.5-0.5B.
2509.01533

2409.19561

What's missing

Ranked by how much each would hurt in review, if the September sweep is heading where it looks like it's heading.

  1. No history of depth-sharing failures. The depth cluster cites no Universal Transformers, ALBERT, Adaptive Computation Time or PonderNet. Every one of them tried parameter sharing across depth, and the field largely moved on. A reviewer will ask what is different now, and “we didn't cite them” is the worst available answer. Fix this first.

  2. No negative or null results anywhere. Sixty-one bookmarks and every paper worked. That biases both the effect sizes you expect and the baselines you'll choose.

  3. No evaluation methodology. The cluster's whole premise is that prior comparisons were unfair. You have SMELT's compute-matching protocol but nothing on benchmark contamination, significance testing in LM evaluation, or ablation design — which is precisely the ground a compute-matched-comparison paper gets attacked on.

  4. RL post-training absent. Defensible if the work is purely architectural. Indefensible the moment a claim touches reasoning capability, because the standard rival explanation for improved reasoning in 2026 is post-training, not architecture.

  5. No mechanistic interpretability. “Depth buys reasoning, parameters buy knowledge” is a claim about what computation happens inside — and the collection has no tooling to look. The neuroscience pair is analogy, not measurement.

Where to start

  2. SMELT 2609.01343 — the methodology to match or beat
  3. Recirculation 2608.17981 — the strongest and most orthogonal recent result
